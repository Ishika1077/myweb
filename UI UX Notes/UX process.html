UX Design Process

1. Define Your Problem (Case Study)

This stage involves clearly identifying the problem you are trying to solve, understanding user needs, and setting project goals. It includes defining target users, pain points, and success criteria.

Example: A food delivery app notices users abandoning carts. The problem is defined as: “Users find the checkout process confusing and time-consuming.”

2. Conduct Research

This stage focuses on gathering insights about users, competitors, and the market through methods like interviews, surveys, and usability studies.

Example: The team conducts user interviews and discovers that users struggle with too many steps during checkout and unclear pricing.

3. Design & Prototype

Designing solutions to the identified problem and creating wireframes or interactive prototypes to visualize how the product will work.

Example: Designers create a simplified one-page checkout prototype with clear pricing and fewer steps using tools like Figma.

4. Test & Gather Feedback

Testing the prototype with real users to identify usability issues and collect feedback on the design.

Example: Users test the new checkout prototype and report that it is faster, but some buttons are hard to notice.

5. Iterate & Refine

Improving the design based on feedback and repeating the process until the solution meets user needs effectively.

Example: Designers update button visibility and layout, then retest until users can complete checkout smoothly without confusion.
